## What I did

- Summarised the US and EU legal bases, incentives and exclusivity rules for orphan drugs
- Mapped the pivotal Skyclarys evidence (the MOXIe trial) to the questions each regulator asked
- Built clear timelines for both pathways
- Framed the patient need, and why the trade-off between speed and certainty matters

## Timelines

- **FDA:** NDA submitted 2020 → refuse-to-file → resubmission 2022 → Priority Review → approval Feb 2023
- **EMA:** MAA 2023 → positive CHMP opinion Dec 2023 → conditional marketing authorisation Feb 2024 → country-by-country access work

## Key points

- First approved therapy for Friedreich's ataxia
- Designations: Orphan, Fast Track and Priority Review in the US; EU orphan designation with conditional approval
- Evidence: improvement on the mFARS scale at 48 weeks, sustained in extension studies

## Takeaway

The EU route can give patients access sooner when unmet need is high. The price is post-marketing data commitments and national HTA and pricing negotiations afterwards. A launch plan has to account for both.
